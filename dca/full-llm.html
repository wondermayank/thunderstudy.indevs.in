Multimedia is a medium of communication that provides information to users in a simple and powerful manner. We are all well familiar with smartphones, computers, and other digital devices. We use these devices for many tasks such as listening to music, watching videos or films, playing games, watching animations or reading books, booking tickets or shopping, etc.

When we use these devices — the images, sound, animation, and the resulting video formed by all of them working together — all of these are multimedia. In other words, the integration of Text, Graphics, Sound, Video, and Animation is called Multimedia.

At present, multimedia is being used extensively in the fields of education, entertainment, business, advertising , and all other sectors.

Multimedia is formed from two words: Multi (meaning many) and Media (meaning a medium of communication through which you can easily convey your message to others). Multimedia is also a part of media, in which several elements are used — such as Text, Image, Audio, Video, Animation, etc.

Multimedia provides information in a very easy and attractive manner. In simple words — multimedia combines text, data, images, graphics, audio, and video and presents them digitally so that information reaches the user in a better and more effective way.

Multimedia plays an extremely useful role in our daily lives. The various forms of multimedia are an integral part of our daily routine and are also very useful in other important tasks.

If you are listening to songs while travelling, playing mobile games, or watching a cartoon show — all of these are examples of multimedia. Music is a form of multimedia that is widely used everywhere and every minute of the day. Listening to music on the radio can also be seen as a form of multimedia.

The main purpose of using multimedia is to communicate easily with people. For example, a radio channel is able to share music with all the people of a state or country. Radio stations also need to play pleasant music that is popular among a large number of listeners, so that the number of advertisements can increase.

If you are a teacher, you can use various forms of multimedia to reach your students. Teachers use various types of multimedia tools to explain difficult subjects to students — through audio/video lectures and presentations. Students want variety in teaching methods; they get bored easily if the teacher always discusses a topic without any variety. So if you want to make the lesson easy and interesting for students, you should use multimedia.

If you are building a company website, it is very important that you keep the information simple, clear, and interesting. Websites are powerful tools for attracting customers to a specific product. If you use different forms of multimedia on your website, visitors will stay longer and browse your entire site. You can choose from good graphics, music files, video files, and even simple mini games to add to your website.

If communication is one of your work goals, you should definitely use some digital media to help you reach your target audience better. You need to understand that people don't like reading long articles and very detailed product information, so it is better to keep it in audio or video form to attract their attention.

In this modern age of communication, there is a great need to promote and spread information — whether it is news or any other information. With the increasing number of TV channels, the need for multimedia in advertising agencies, event management companies, and the media industry has truly grown. Multimedia is most widely used in entertainment and gaming, and besides these, it is also used in every industry such as Hospitality, Aviation, Banking, Insurance, Science & Technology, etc.

Multimedia is a collection of many elements such as Text, Audio, Graphics, Video, and Animation . All elements of multimedia are delivered better and more effectively to users through computers. A detailed description of multimedia elements is as follows:

5.1 Text

Text is used in multimedia mostly for headings, headlines, menus, etc. Text presents information through letters, numbers, and special characters. The most commonly used software for viewing text files are Microsoft Word, Notepad, WordPad, etc. Most text files are used with extensions like .DOC, .TXT , etc.

5.2 Audio

Audio means sound waves that we can hear. Audio is an analogue signal, which is stored in a computer by converting it to a digital signal with the help of an ADC (Analog to Digital Converter) . Digital Audio Data prepared by the computer can be heard via speakers or headphones. Audio includes speech, music, etc.

5.3 Graphics

Every multimedia presentation is based on graphics. The use of graphics in multimedia makes it possible to present any subject more effectively in visual form. Commonly used graphic viewing software includes Windows Picture Manager, Picasa Photo Viewer, Internet Explorer, etc. Commonly used graphics editing software includes Adobe Photoshop, CorelDRAW, InDesign and many others, through which graphics can be easily edited and made effective and attractive.

5.4 Video

Video means moving pictures with sound. It is the best way to communicate with each other. In multimedia, it is used to present information more effectively and saves a large amount of time. Commonly used video editing software includes Adobe Premiere, VideoPad and many others. Many mobile apps are also available for editing. Players: QuickTime, Real Player, Windows Media Player.

5.5 Animation

A collection of still graphic images displayed one after another in such a way as to give the impression of motion in the picture is called Animation . Animation has a series of pictures in which each picture is replaced by the next after a certain interval so that the picture appears to be moving.

An essential quality of a multimedia computer system is that it should be capable of capturing different types of media, editing them, using video/audio, storing all of them, and sending all types of data from one computer to another via modem or network.

Since many types of media are included together in multimedia, special hardware and software are required for using multimedia and making multimedia projects. Mobile devices, laptops, or desktop computers can all be used to consume multimedia, but for creating multimedia-related projects, designers generally use high-configuration desktop computers.

6.2.1 CPU (Central Processing Unit)

The Central Processing Unit is the most important part of a computer — it is the part where all tasks are processed and synchronised. The computer's capacity is measured by the speed of data processing. For multimedia use, Pentium Processor IV or higher is preferred.

6.2.2 Graphics Card

A graphics card is connected to the computer's motherboard. When you view pictures or video on a computer, or play games, the graphics card works to display them better. A graphics card is the hardware component that displays all graphics on the monitor. It converts graphical data into signals so the monitor can understand it. It is also known as a Video Card, Graphic Adapter, or Display Adapter.

Most computers have integrated graphics processing in the CPU for normal use (watching pictures, videos). But for high-graphics software like gaming or video editing, an additional graphics card with its own RAM and processor — called a GPU (Graphical Processing Unit) — needs to be installed.

6.2.3 Memory & Storage Devices

You need memory to store production files, original audio and video clips, edited audio/video segments, and various files used during multimedia production, as well as backup of your project files.

6.2.4 Input Devices

  • Keyboard: The most common and popular input device. Layout is like a traditional typewriter with additional keys. Available in 84, 101/102, 104, or 108 key variants. Special multimedia keyboards are available for volume control and gaming.
  • Mouse: An optical computer mouse uses a light source (LED) and a light detector that detects mouse movement relative to the surface. It has two buttons (left and right) and a scroll bar in the middle. Used to control cursor position; cannot enter text.
  • Joystick: A pointing device used to move the cursor position on the monitor screen. It is a stick with round balls at both lower and upper ends. The lower ball moves in a socket. Can be moved in all four directions. Mainly used in CAD and computer games.
  • Light Pen: A pointing device similar to a pen. Used to select displayed menu items or draw pictures on the monitor screen. Contains a photocell and optical system in a small tube. When the light pen is placed on the screen, the photocell sensing element detects the screen location and sends the signal to CPU. Mainly used with design tablets for designing on a computer.
  • Digitizer: A hardware device that receives analogue information (like sound or light) and records it digitally. The process is called digitisation. Example: A digital camera is a digitiser — light enters through the lens, hardware and software inside the camera converts it to binary data and stores it as an image file.
  • Scanner: An input device that works like a photocopying machine. Used when information is available on paper and needs to be transferred to the computer's hard disk for modification. Captures images from the source, converts them to digital form that can be stored on disk, and can be edited before printing. Many mobile applications also offer scanning facilities.
  • Microphone: An input device for inputting sound, stored digitally. Used alone or integrated with headphones. Applications include adding voice to multimedia presentations or mixing music.
  • Speaker: An output device for producing sound stored digitally. Used for adding audio to multimedia presentations or for film screening. All modern computer monitors have integrated speakers; mobile devices also have built-in speakers.
  • Digital Camera: An input device for inputting images, stored digitally. Used for adding images to multimedia presentations or personal purposes.
  • Digital Video Camera: An input device for inputting pictures/video, stored digitally. Used for adding video to multimedia presentations or personal purposes.

6.2.5 Output Devices

  • Flat-Panel Display Monitor: Refers to a class of video devices with less volume, weight, and power requirement than CRTs. Can be hung on walls or worn on the wrist. Divided into: (i) Emissive Displays — convert electrical energy to light (e.g., Plasma Panels, LEDs); (ii) Non-Emissive Displays — use optical effects to convert sunlight/light from another source into graphics patterns (e.g., LCD).
  • Printer: An electronic device for printing digital information on paper. (i) Dot Matrix Printer — prints characters by joining dots; has a pin matrix on the print head; (ii) Laser Printer — produces high-quality images for office/business purposes using a laser beam on a photo-sensitive drum.
  • Image Projector: An output device used to project information from the computer onto a large screen so a group of people can see it simultaneously. A presenter first creates a presentation on the computer, then the projector is plugged into the system to present to a large audience.
  • Speaker & Sound Card: A computer needs both a sound card and speakers to hear audio. Most motherboards provide an on-board sound card. The main function of the sound card is to convert the digital sound signal into analogue for speakers, which can be increased or decreased in volume. For gaming technology, Virtual Reality headsets are also included.

Text is an important component of multimedia applications — it is the easiest multimedia element and is also effective in exchanging information. Text consists of characters that are used to form words , sentences , and paragraphs . It is possible to provide information only through text, but text is commonly used only for giving general information.

If proper and correct attention is not given to the use of text, the communication of a multimedia application cannot be done properly. The proper presentation of text refers to the type of text and its other properties such as font, font size, text colour , etc.

2.1.1 Definition of Text

Text is the easiest to change. Where very effective communication is happening in the form of text, the multimedia designer should not try to convert the text into an image or sound. Text is an important component used in many multimedia applications. They are characters used to form Words, Sentences, and Paragraphs.

Text in multimedia presentations can deliver a large amount of information using very little storage space. The main features of text include paragraph styling, character styling, font family , and font size .

  • Plain Text — Unformatted, same-looking characters throughout
  • Formatted Text — Can be changed using font parameters (bold, italic, underline, size, colour)
  • Hypertext — Text used in websites linked to other pages/files

2.2.1 Plain Text

Plain text is a text file or a collection of documents that contains only text. This text is written in the same way throughout — it does not have any special properties such as being written in different colours, being written differently, being bold or underlined, etc.

In other words, unlike a rich text document, a plain text file does not have bold text, fonts, larger font sizes, or any other special text formatting . On Microsoft Windows computers, most plain text files are available with the file extension .txt . To view a plain text file, a text editor like Microsoft Notepad is used.

Plain text is the most portable format because it supports almost every application on every machine.

2.2.2 Formatted Text

Formatted text is text that is displayed in a specific, designated style. In computer applications, formatting data is associated with text data to create formatted text. How formatted text is created and displayed depends on the operating system and application software used on the computer.

Formatted text can be any text that has special formatting, such as font size, font colour, bold, italic , etc. When copying text, formatted text is any text that retains its settings from where it was copied. Text formatting data can be qualitative (e.g., font family) or quantitative (e.g., font size, or colour). Styles of emphasis (bold, italic) or notation (strikethrough, superscript) can be used in text formatting. The purpose of formatted text is to enhance the presentation of information.

2.2.3 Best Practices for Text in Multimedia Presentations

  • Using only uppercase or only lowercase is worse than using them in mixed form — mixed use makes any text easier to read.
  • Any kind of warning or special message should be written completely in uppercase.
  • While writing text, it is appropriate that no sentence is more than 60 characters long — text can be read at a fast pace. Using short sentences and ensuring a sentence is not split across two pages should be kept in mind.
  • Only one-third of the display should be filled with text — text can be made good for reading and writing through margin, line spacing, etc.
  • On a computer screen, a minimum of 12-point text is appropriate for reading. In other multimedia uses, the text size is determined by the display size.

2.2.5 Rich Text Format (RTF)

RTF is a text file format used by all Microsoft products such as Word and Office. They can be read by almost all word processing software. Presented and documented by Microsoft, Rich Text Format (RTF) describes a method for encoding formatted text and graphics for use within applications.

HTML stands for HyperText Markup Language, the most widely used language on the web for developing web pages. HTML was created by Berners-Lee at the end of 1991, but "HTML 2.0" was the first standard HTML specification published in 1995 .

HTML is the most basic building block of the web. It defines the meaning and structure of web content. "HyperText" refers to links that connect web pages to one another, either within a single website or between websites. Links are a fundamental aspect of the web.

3.1 Mono vs Stereo Audio Sound

Mono and stereo audio provide quite different listening experiences. Stereo is the most common format for home audio systems and recorded music, while mono sound provides different benefits for users who want high-quality experiences with older recordings.

3.2 Mono Sound (Detail)

Monophonic or "mono" sound is produced by routing a single signal channel in amplifiers to one or more speakers. Even if you use two speakers, a mono signal still produces the exact and same sound level in each speaker — therefore, unlike stereo speakers, the audio quality of a mono sound system is not very good. Through headphones, mono sound produces a "cluster" of music without experiencing audio imaging.

Recording and reproduction of mono sound is less expensive and easier to record, requiring only basic equipment. Mono sound system audio signals are routed through a single channel.

Stereo sound is a process of sound production that simulates audible sound as if coming from different directions. Recording and reproduction costs for stereo sound are more expensive. Stereo sound systems are used in cinema, television, music players, FM radio stations .

Both analog and digital signals are used to transmit information, usually through electric signals. In both these technologies, information — such as any audio or video — is converted into electric signals. The difference between analog and digital technologies is that in analog technology, information is converted into electric pulses of varying amplitudes. In digital technology, information is translated into binary format (zero or one), where each bit represents two distinct amplitudes.

Both analog and digital are techniques of encoding information. Digital computers and other electronic devices use information in sequences of 1s and 0s — this data is then read by the electronic device and interpreted as words, images, or sounds. Analog, on the other hand, means a continuous chain of electrical waves, which provide different values with different parameters.

4.1 Analog Signal (Detail)

An analog signal is a continuous signal that represents physical measurements. It is characterised by sine waves and uses a continuous range of values to describe information. The analog signal can only be used in analog devices and is most suited for audio and video transmission. Its bandwidth processing can be done in real time and consumes less bandwidth. Analog instruments generally have a scale and give observational errors.

4.2 Digital Signal (Detail)

A digital signal is a signal used to represent data in a sequence of discrete values. At any time it can take only one number from a limited number of values (0 or 1). Like analog, digital signals transfer data, although the way to transfer data is slightly different. These signals are discrete, not continuous. A digital signal transfers data as binary because a digital signal represents all information only in bits.

Every digital signal has amplitude, frequency , and phase like analog signals. This signal is also defined by bit interval and bit rate . Bit interval is the time it takes to transfer one bit; bit rate is the frequency of bit interval. Digital signals are more resistant to noise during transmission and are more reliable and simpler to transmit than analog signals.

Information or data on computers and electronic devices is stored as files. Text, audio, video — all store information in different file formats. A sound file format is a file format for storing audio on a computer. It defines the quality and characteristics of audio data. Different types of sound file formats are used based on the application.

Sound file formats are mainly divided into three parts:

5.1 Uncompressed Audio Formats

PCM describes an irregular (raw) analog audio signal in digital form. To convert an analog signal to a digital signal, it has to be recorded at a special interval. Therefore it has a sampling rate and bit rate (bits used to describe each sample). It is an accurate interpretation of analog sound and does not include file compression. It is the most common audio format used in CDs and DVDs.

WAV was developed in 1991 by Microsoft and IBM . It is simply a Windows container for audio formats, meaning a WAV file can contain compressed audio. Most WAV files contain uncompressed audio in PCM format. Compatible with both Windows and Mac.

AIFF was developed by Apple for Mac systems in 1988 . Like WAV files, AIFF files can contain many types of audio. They contain uncompressed audio in PCM format. Compatible with both Windows and Mac.

5.2 Lossy Compressed Formats

This is a form of compression — audio files can be stored in smaller form by compressing them. Smaller files are easy to send over the internet or through other mediums, and take less time. During the compression process, some data is lost. In lossy compression, the quality of data is compromised — but the difference in quality is generally not audible.

MP3 is the most popular audio file format for music files, released in 1993 . The main purpose of MP3 is to remove all sounds that are more or less noticeable by human ears, so MP3 music file sizes are smaller. MP3 is like a universal format compatible with almost every device.

AAC was developed in 1997 after MP3. The compression algorithm used by AAC is much more complex and advanced than MP3. When a particular audio file in MP3 and AAC format is compared at the same bit rate, AAC generally gets better sound quality. It is the standard audio compression method used by YouTube, Android, iOS, iTunes , and PlayStations.

WMA was released in 1999 . It was designed to overcome some drawbacks of the MP3 compression method. In terms of quality it is better than MP3. However, WMA is not widely used.

5.3 Lossless Compressed Formats

This method reduces the file size without any loss in quality. However, lossless compression is not as good as lossy compression because the file size in lossless compression is 2 to 3 times larger than lossy compression, so it can be used less from the perspective of file transport.

FLAC can compress the source file up to 50% without losing data. It is the most popular in its category and is open-source.

ALAC was developed by Apple. It was launched in 2004 , but in 2011 Apple made the codec open source and royalty-free.

MIDI by itself does not produce any sound — it only sends messages. MIDI instruments understand these messages and produce sound. These instruments can be any hardware like an electronic keyboard or synthesizer, or any software of that environment like GarageBand, Digital Performer .

What MIDI Data Contains

MIDI data contains several types of information. For example, pressing a single key on a synthesizer transmits:

  • The note played
  • Velocity (how hard the note is pressed)
  • How long the note was played

If multiple notes are played simultaneously, MIDI data is transmitted for all notes at once. Other data that can be sent over a MIDI connection includes: Instrument ID, sustain pedal timing, controller information such as pitch bend and vibrato.

MIDI Recording Process

When a synthesizer is connected to a computer via a MIDI connection, the notes to be played can be recorded by DAW software in MIDI format. MIDI data can be played back by sending the recorded MIDI notes to the keyboard, which outputs them as audio samples such as piano or drums. Most DAW software supports MIDI editing, allowing you to adjust the timing and velocity of individual notes, change pitch, remove notes, or add new notes.

MIDI Connectivity

Originally, MIDI connections used a MIDI cable connected to a 5-pin MIDI port on each device. Now most MIDI instruments have standard computer interfaces such as USB or Thunderbolt ports . These modern interfaces provide more bandwidth than traditional MIDI ports, allowing tracks with more data to be transmitted at once.

3.9 Aadhaar

Aadhaar is issued to residents of India after completing the verification process determined by the UIDAI authority. Aadhaar number is a 12-digit random number . Achieved through the process of demographic and biometric de-duplication. The de-duplication process verifies whether the resident is already in the UIDAI database. Aadhaar provides nation-wide portability because it can be authenticated online anywhere. This is important because millions of Indians move from one state to another or from rural areas to urban centres.

When two or more computing devices share data or resources with each other, it is called a Computer Network . These computing devices can be computers, laptops, smartphones, tablet devices, or any other computing device. To create a computer network, specific hardware and software are both required, which help build the network system.

Computing devices use a medium to connect with each other in a network. This medium can be wired or wireless . In a wired network, computing devices share data through cables; in wireless networks, no cable is needed.

Wireless networks use radio waves to connect devices. The main types of wireless networks are:

Mobiles, laptops, tablet computers, internet audio systems, gaming consoles, and internet-enabled home appliances can be connected via WLAN. The connection is made via radio waves through transmitters and receivers built into these devices or via antennas. WLAN keeps users connected to the network while remaining within a limited area. Through a gateway, a WLAN can also provide a connection to the broader internet.

The IEEE 802.11 family of standards defines technologies for wireless LANs. The 802.11 standard uses the Ethernet protocol and CSMA/CA (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance) for path sharing. It also uses a Wired Equivalent Privacy (WEP) encryption algorithm.

4.1.1 Advantages of WLAN

Within radio coverage, nodes can communicate without interruption. Radio waves can pass through walls — the sender and receiver can be anywhere without disruption.

Wired networks need a wiring plan, but wireless ad-hoc networks require no planning at all.

Wireless devices can be moved from place to place within a defined range, allowing design of independent, small, pocket-sized devices. Wired devices don't offer this portability.

No planned cabling means wireless networks remain functional during natural disasters like earthquakes and floods — unlike wired networks which go completely down.

Setting up and maintaining a WLAN costs less on average than a wired LAN. Adding more users doesn't increase costs, and cabling and installation labor is eliminated.

WLAN is easy to use. Users need very little or no technical knowledge to take advantage of WLAN.

Bluetooth usage has increased enormously today. Transferring data from smartphones and laptops to speakers, car music systems, and Bluetooth earphones is common for almost everyone.

Bluetooth is a wireless communication standard that allows electronic devices to connect and interact with each other. It is found in smartphones, loudspeakers, laptops, and many gadgets. Bluetooth does not depend on Wi-Fi, mobile data, or cell networks — as long as devices are Bluetooth-compatible and close to each other, they can communicate wirelessly in two directions. A Bluetooth-enabled device equipped with a Bluetooth receiver can link and communicate with up to seven nearest Bluetooth devices .

4.2.1 Bluetooth Architecture

Bluetooth architecture defines two types of networks:

A Piconet is a Bluetooth network with one primary (master) node and seven active secondary (slave) nodes, working on a master/slave model. The master device acts as coordinator — all devices send to and receive information from the master to communicate with each other.

  • Maximum 8 active nodes (1 master + 7 slaves) within 10 metres.
  • Each piconet can have only one master station.
  • Communication between primary and secondary can be one-to-one or one-to-many.
  • A slave cannot communicate directly with another slave.

Two or more interconnected piconets form a Scatternet . This allows more than eight devices to communicate. A member of one piconet (master or slave) can act as a slave in another piconet. A device that is a slave in one piconet and master in another is called a Bridge Slave . A station cannot be master in two piconets simultaneously.

4.2.2 Advantages of Bluetooth

  • Low installation cost — Bluetooth is usually built into electronic gadgets.
  • Easy to use — connectivity without internet.

  • Can instantly create ad-hoc connections without any wires.

Old wireless systems used powerful transmitters that served an entire area, requiring very high power. Current wireless networking uses cellular systems . Cellular networks use low power, transmit data with more transmitters over short distances. The coverage area is divided into cells — each cell has its own antenna for transmitting signals and its own frequency.

In simple terms: a cell is a geographical area covered by a cellular telephone transmitter. The transmitter facility itself is called a cell site . A cell can be square or hexagonal in shape. Frequency reuse allows the same radio frequency to be used within a given area with minimum interference between cells.

4.17 MTSO – Mobile Telephone Switching Office

The MTSO is the mobile equivalent of a public switched telephone network (PSTN) central office. It contains switching equipment to route mobile phone calls and control units for the cell sites connected to the Mobile Switching Center (MSC). MTSO handles call interconnection with local and long-distance landline telephone companies, billing, registration, authentication, location updates, and call routing.

4.17.1 Handoff in Mobile Connection

Conditions that Trigger Handoff

  • A subscriber moves out of one cell's coverage area and enters another cell's coverage.
  • A cell reaches its pre-configured capacity — some calls are transferred to neighbouring cells.
  • Cells are subdivided into microcells — handoff occurs when duties transfer between large and small cells.
  • Interference in calls using the same frequency.

4.17.3 Types of Handoff

  • Actual break in connection when switching cells.
  • Radio link to current cell is cut before establishing link with next cell.
  • Usually an inter-frequency handoff.
  • "Break before Make" policy.
  • At least one link is maintained when adding/removing radio links.
  • No break during handoff — seamless experience.
  • Usually adopted at co-located sites.
  • "Make before Break" policy.

4.17.4 Base Station

A base station is a fixed communication location and part of the network's wireless telephone system. It transmits information from the transmitting unit and receives from the receiving unit (such as mobile phones). Often known as a cell site, a base station allows mobile phones to work in a local area as long as it is connected to a mobile or wireless service provider.

Desktop Publishing (DTP) refers to a publishing process that can be edited on a desk itself. That is, performing publishing work through equipment placed on a desk is called Desktop Publishing. This work is done through a computer and connected equipment such as printers and scanners.

In this system, the creation of text, arranging it on a page, and making images etc. is done on a computer. Finally, using a laser printer, printing is done on butter paper or master via the printer. This butter paper or master is then used in printing machines.

Three Main Equipment Requirements for DTP

Types of Publishing Using DTP

  • Commercial Publishing: Newspapers, magazines, web pages and other visual documentation.
  • Office Publishing: Brochures, flyers, posters, booklets, business cards, letterhead forms, financial documents, HR documents, invoices, inventory sheets, memos, and labels.
  • Personal Publishing: Projects, postcards, greeting cards, invitation letters, resumes, cover letters, and portfolios.

History of DTP

In the mid-1980s, Apple's PostScript desktop printer LaserWriter and the development of Aldus PageMaker for Apple Macintosh computers sparked the desktop publishing revolution.

The credit for DTP's invention goes to Apple Computer's Steve Jobs, while Paul Brainerd, founder of Aldus Corporation, is generally credited with coining the phrase "Desktop Publishing."

Brief Timeline

Publishing technology is deeply connected to our daily lives. From newspapers, pamphlets, books, magazines, visiting cards, advertisements, hoardings, calendars, posters, to restaurant menu cards — the designing and printing of all these use DTP.

Importance of Desktop Publishing

  • Documents look high quality and beautiful due to desktop publishing and graphic design.
  • If used properly, scattered data can be organized neatly.
  • It is a communication-enhancing tool that enables quick creation of written and electronic documents.
  • It allows the user to set text and graphics on screen, helping in the creation of effective and attractive documents.

Benefits of Desktop Publishing

Today there is not much difference between available desktop publishing and word processing software because word processing software keeps adding new page layouting facilities. However, there are some fundamental differences:

DTP allows the computer user to combine text and image files into a single document. The user designs a page that looks like a page in a book or journal. It is arranged in several pieces as different news items on a page to give the form of a newspaper.

A newspaper is a classic use of desktop publishing. Professional newspaper editors use text and pictures made by journalists and photographers many miles away and combine them with diagrams and headlines made in the office to create a newspaper.

Desktop publishers and graphic designers generally use four types of software:

1. Word Processing Software

Used for typing, editing text and checking spelling and grammar. Text prepared in word processing software is generally imported into page-layout software for formatting.

2. Page Layout Software

Closely associated with desktop publishing. Allows integration of text and images on the page, easy rearrangement of page elements, and creation of artistic layouts. Helpful for publications like newspapers and books.

Key DTP Software Details

The specific style of text presentation is called text formatting. DTP software has unique text formatting facilities — including the spacing between letters, between words, between lines and between paragraphs.

Typography

The word Typography comes from the Greek words 'typos' (sign, picture) and 'grapho' (I write). In Hindi, typography is called mudran kala (printing art). It is the art and technique of arranging printing to make written language legible, readable, and appealing when displayed.

Typography involves the selection of typeface, point size, line length, leading (line spacing), space between letter groups (tracking) and space between letter pairs (kerning).

Font

A font is a collection of letters of the same design. These include lowercase and uppercase letters, numbers, punctuation marks and symbols. Changing the font changes how text looks and feels.

Serif vs Sans-Serif Fonts

Font Size (Point Size)

In typography, point is the smallest unit of measurement. Used to measure font size, leading, and other items on a printed page. In DTP, a point is defined as 1⁄72 of an international inch (approximately 0.353 mm). One inch = approximately 72 points.

Text Formatting Controls

Printing processes can be understood differently by the method of image transfer and the type of image carrier process. Based on the process, the printed image is transferred either directly or indirectly onto the printing surface.

Lithography

Lithography is a printing method whose theoretical basis is that oil and water do not mix. It uses oil-based ink on a stone (lithographic limestone) or a metal plate with a granular surface. The printer works on a different stone or plate for each color. Traditionally this surface was specially prepared limestone, but today granular aluminum printing plates and original stones are used.

Flexography

In flexography, the material to be printed is on the surface of a rubber printing plate. Ink is applied to this plate and that ink image is later transferred to the printing surface. The process can be used for printing on paper as well as plastic, metal, and other materials. Flexo is mainly used for packaging and labels and to some extent for newspapers also.

Gravure Printing

Also known as Rotogravure. This is a technique in which an image is engraved in a printing cylinder. Ink is applied to that cylinder and later this ink transfers to paper. Gravure is used for high volume work such as newspapers, magazines, and packaging. It is slowly losing market share due to popularity of offset in publication printing and flexo in packaging applications.

Offset Printing

The most widely used printing technique for a wide range of products like business cards, letterheads, catalogs, books/booklets, business forms, flyers, brochures, calendars, invitation letters, newspapers, stationery, and posters. It is not only the highest quality printing process, it also provides the best price per piece.

Screen Printing

As the name implies, this printing technique depends on a screen. Some areas of this screen are coated with non-permeable material. Ink can be pushed through the remaining open spaces of the screen onto the surface of cloth or paper. The advantage is that the printing surface doesn't need to be flat — printing can be done on paper, cloth, glass, ceramics, wood and metal surfaces.

A laser printer is a type of printer that uses a laser beam to create an image on a drum. Also known as a page printer because it prints the entire page at once. It uses toner powder instead of ink for printing. Laser printer was first developed in 1971 by Gary Starkweather at Xerox PARC.

How a Laser Printer Prints (Step by Step)

Types of Laser Printers

  • Personal Laser Printer: Very small, designed to fit on a desk with other computer equipment. Can be connected to computer for single-user use. Print on one side of paper only.
  • Office Laser Printer: Slightly larger than personal printers but still fit comfortably on a desk. Can be used by one person or shared by a small group of users.
  • Workgroup Laser Printer: Specifically designed for sharing among multiple users on a network. Often large enough to be placed on the floor. Designed to run continuously without operator intervention for long periods.
  • Production Laser Printer: High-speed printers operated by an operator. Very large, floor-standing equipment, often requiring three-phase electricity. Sometimes requires air-conditioned "computer room" environment. Designed for round-the-clock use.
  • Color Laser Printer: A relatively new development. Uses four colors (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Black) like offset printers. Separate cartridges for each of the four colors. Prints four times on paper — each time with a different color.

Advantages of Laser Printers

  • Speed — laser beam moves very fast, prints more pages per minute (PPM) than inkjet printers
  • Accuracy — laser beam has uniform diameter, writes more precisely, ink doesn't spread
  • High-resolution output — up to 1200 dpi
  • Lower running costs — toner is cheaper and lasts longer than inkjet cartridges
  • Less noise during printing
  • Less maintenance required

Uses of Laser Printers

Laser printers are continuously used in commercial work as they are reliable and high-quality. Common commercial uses include company stationery, making labels, and printing company flyers and brochures. In the publishing industry they are used both for continuous low-quality proofs and for high-quality masters — for printing newspapers, magazines, books, posters, hoardings, etc.
